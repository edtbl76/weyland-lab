# ADKAR Model

## What It Is
A goal-oriented change management model developed by Jeff Hiatt at Prosci that describes the five building blocks individuals must develop to successfully adopt and sustain a change: Awareness (of the need to change), Desire (to participate and support the change), Knowledge (of how to change), Ability (to implement the skills and behaviors), and Reinforcement (to sustain the change). Unlike process-oriented change frameworks (Kotter's 8 Steps), ADKAR focuses on the individual — change happens only when every person affected completes all five stages. The model is used both diagnostically (identifying where change is stalling) and prescriptively (designing interventions for each stage).

## When to Use
- Planning the change management track of a technology transformation program
- Diagnosing why a previously deployed system isn't being adopted
- When resistance to a technology change is present but not well understood — ADKAR identifies which stage is blocking individual adoption
- Designing training, communication, and support plans for technology rollouts
- Change readiness assessment before a major program launch

## Key Concepts
- **Awareness**: Do people understand why the change is happening? What is driving it? What happens if we don't change? Insufficient Awareness is the first barrier — people resist what they don't understand. Interventions: executive communication, business case sharing, "burning platform" messaging
- **Desire**: Do people want to make the change? Even when they understand it, people may not want to change — due to personal interest conflicts, loss of status, distrust of the organization, or loyalty to the current state. Interventions: manager engagement, addressing WIIFM ("what's in it for me"), incentive alignment
- **Knowledge**: Do people know how to change? Training, job aids, documentation, coaching. Knowledge interventions are often where organizations overspend — launching training before Desire is built ensures poor attendance and retention
- **Ability**: Can people perform the new behaviors consistently? Knowledge is theoretical; Ability requires practice in the actual environment. Interventions: on-the-job coaching, performance support tools, safe environments to practice
- **Reinforcement**: What sustains the change after the program is over? Without reinforcement, people revert to old behaviors. Interventions: performance management, recognition, ongoing coaching, accountability mechanisms, removing the old system
- **Diagnostic Power**: ADKAR's primary value is diagnostic. When adoption is stalling, identifying which stage is blocked directs the intervention. Organizations often respond to low adoption with more training (Knowledge intervention) when the real block is Desire
- **Manager Involvement**: ADKAR research consistently identifies direct managers as the most influential factor in individual change success. Programs that engage managers as change agents — not just recipients of communication — outperform those that treat change management as a communications function
- **ADKAR vs. Kotter**: Kotter describes the organizational change process from the leader's perspective; ADKAR describes the individual change journey from the employee's perspective. They are complementary — Kotter at the organizational level, ADKAR at the individual level

## Method Application
Method builds an ADKAR-based change management plan alongside every major technology delivery program. Technology without adoption is wasted investment. The ADKAR plan establishes interventions for each stage, timed to delivery milestones — Awareness before design decisions, Desire before pilot launch, Knowledge and Ability before go-live, Reinforcement in the post-go-live period.

## Consulting Insight
🎯 **Consulting Tool — ADKAR**: The most common change management failure is a training-heavy plan that ignores Awareness and Desire. Organizations schedule training for week 11 when users haven't been told why the change is happening until week 10. By the time knowledge is delivered, resistance has solidified into avoidance. The ADKAR sequence is not a suggestion — each stage must be substantially complete before the next one is effective. If a user doesn't want to change, no amount of training will produce lasting ability. → `consulting-tools-repository/adkar.md`
