# North Star Metric

## What It Is
A single metric that best captures the core value a product delivers to its customers — used as the primary long-term indicator of product success and business health. The North Star Metric (NSM) concept, associated with Sean Ellis and widely adopted in growth-oriented product organizations, argues that companies succeed by optimizing for customer value delivery, and that one metric best represents that value at a given stage of the product's lifecycle. The NSM sits above feature-level metrics and OKR Key Results as the organizing principle for product strategy — every team's work should connect to moving the North Star.

## When to Use
- Establishing product success criteria before a program begins
- When a client is measuring success through vanity metrics (downloads, page views) rather than value delivery
- Aligning multiple teams around a single directional metric
- Evaluating whether a proposed feature or initiative connects to core product value
- Growth strategy conversations: what does the product need to do more of to scale?

## Key Concepts
- **Properties of a Good NSM**: Captures delivered value (not just activity), leads revenue (is a leading indicator rather than lagging), measurable (quantifiable with available data), understandable (every team member can explain it), actionable (teams can influence it through their work)
- **NSM Examples by Business Model**:
  - SaaS: Monthly Active Users, Weekly Engaged Users, Seats Activated
  - Marketplace: Gross Merchandise Value, Successful Transactions per Month
  - Media/Content: Content Consumed per User per Week, Returning Readers per Month
  - Enterprise: Workflows Automated, Reports Generated, Data Processed
- **Leading vs. Lagging**: Revenue is a lagging indicator — it follows value delivery. The NSM should be a leading indicator that predicts revenue. Teams that optimize for revenue directly often sacrifice the customer value that produces it
- **NSM vs. KPIs**: KPIs measure operational health across dimensions; the NSM is the single metric that everything else serves. A company can have 30 KPIs but should have one NSM
- **NSM Decomposition**: The NSM is decomposed into Input Metrics — the drivers that move it. Teams own input metrics, not the NSM itself. Example: NSM = Weekly Active Users; Input Metrics = new user activation rate, feature adoption rate, session frequency, churn rate
- **NSM Lifecycle**: The right NSM changes as the business matures. Early-stage: activation and engagement. Growth-stage: retention and expansion. Mature-stage: revenue efficiency and NPS. Reassess annually
- **Countermetrics**: Pair the NSM with a countermetric that prevents gaming. Optimizing for active users without a countermetric can inflate through poor-quality engagement. Add a quality signal: active users who complete a core workflow, not just log in

## Method Application
Method surfaces the NSM question early in client engagements: "What is the one metric that, if it improved consistently, would indicate the technology program succeeded?" Clients without a clear answer often have programs scoped around delivery outputs rather than business outcomes. Establishing the NSM before scoping connects every workstream to a measurable business result.

## Consulting Insight
🎯 **Consulting Tool — North Star Metric**: The diagnostic question is not "what is your NSM?" — it's "does your team's work move it?" When teams can't explain how their sprint's work connects to the NSM, it's a signal that the roadmap has drifted from product strategy. The NSM is a forcing function for prioritization: if an initiative doesn't move the North Star directly or through a known input metric, it needs a better justification or a lower priority. → `consulting-tools-repository/north-star-metric.md`
