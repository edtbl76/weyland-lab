# How Might We (HMW)

## What It Is
A reframing technique originally developed at Procter & Gamble and popularized by IDEO and Google Ventures that transforms problem observations and pain points into open-ended design questions using the phrase "How Might We...?" The three words are deliberate: "How" assumes a solution exists; "Might" keeps options open (it's exploratory, not prescriptive); "We" signals collective ownership. HMW questions sit at the transition between research synthesis and ideation — converting what you've learned about a problem into an invitation to generate solutions. They are broad enough to allow creative exploration but focused enough to be actionable.

## When to Use
- Transitioning from research synthesis (affinity maps, journey maps) to ideation
- Day 1 of a Design Sprint: reframing problem observations before solution sketching
- Requirements workshops: converting stakeholder complaints into design challenges
- Whenever a team is stuck in problem description and needs to pivot to solution generation
- Aligning a team on what they're trying to solve before brainstorming begins

## Key Concepts
- **The Reframe Mechanism**: HMW takes a negative observation and turns it into a positive design challenge. "Users can't find what they're looking for" → "How might we make discovery intuitive for first-time users?" The reframe is not cosmetic — it changes the cognitive orientation from diagnosis to possibility
- **Scope Calibration**: HMW questions can be too broad ("How might we improve healthcare?") or too narrow ("How might we add a search filter to the results page?"). Well-scoped HMWs are specific enough to be actionable but broad enough to allow multiple solution directions. When a HMW has only one obvious answer, it's too narrow
- **HMW Generation Process**:
  1. Gather research outputs — interview notes, journey maps, observation notes
  2. Identify insights and pain points (often via affinity mapping)
  3. For each significant insight, write one or more HMW questions
  4. Review HMWs for scope — narrow if too broad, broaden if too narrow
  5. Vote or select the most promising HMWs to carry into ideation
- **Quantity Over Quality in Generation**: When generating HMWs, produce many before evaluating any. The goal is to create a rich set of possible design challenges; selection comes after generation
- **HMW as Shared Language**: A set of HMW questions agreed on by the team creates a shared definition of what the workshop or sprint is trying to solve. Teams that skip this step often end up generating solutions to different problems in the same ideation session
- **HMW in Event Storming**: Hotspots identified in Event Storming — areas of confusion, risk, or disagreement — are natural sources of HMW questions. The HMW reframes the hotspot as a design challenge

## Method Application
Method uses HMW questions as the standard transition between discovery and design across all workshop types. After a research readout or a journey map walkthrough, before opening ideation, facilitators convert the key findings into HMW questions. This prevents the session from jumping to solutions before the team has aligned on what problems they're solving.

## Consulting Insight
🎯 **Consulting Tool — How Might We**: HMW's facilitation power is in what it prevents — teams generating solutions to different problems. When a group of six people starts ideating without shared problem framing, they produce six sets of solutions to six slightly different problems. The five minutes spent converting research insights into HMW questions and selecting the two to address produces alignment that would take an hour of discussion to achieve through argument.
