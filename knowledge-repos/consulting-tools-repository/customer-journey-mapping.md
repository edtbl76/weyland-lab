# Customer Journey Mapping

## What It Is
A visualization technique that maps a customer's end-to-end experience with a product, service, or organization across time — capturing their actions, thoughts, emotions, touchpoints, and pain points at each stage of the journey. Journey maps make visible the full customer experience that is often fragmented across organizational silos — the customer's experience is continuous even when the organization's view of it is not. The map creates shared empathy across teams who each own a slice of the experience but rarely see the whole. Journey maps are both a research artifact (documenting what is) and a design tool (imagining what could be).

## When to Use
- Discovery phase: understanding the current-state customer experience before designing solutions
- When multiple teams own different parts of a customer experience and need a shared view
- Identifying customer pain points that technology investment should address
- Requirements analysis: grounding functional requirements in customer moments rather than system functions
- Presenting the case for investment: showing stakeholders where customers struggle in visceral, specific terms

## Key Concepts
- **Journey Stages**: The high-level phases a customer moves through — Awareness, Consideration, Purchase, Onboarding, Usage, Support, Renewal/Churn are common for SaaS; vary by industry and product
- **Customer Actions**: What the customer is doing at each stage — tasks, channels, decisions, tools they use
- **Thoughts**: What the customer is thinking — questions they have, assumptions they're making, information they're seeking
- **Emotions**: How the customer feels — frustration, confusion, delight, anxiety, confidence. Often shown as a curve across the journey. The emotional dips are where pain points live
- **Touchpoints**: Every interaction point between the customer and the organization — website, email, app, phone, in-person, third-party channel. Each touchpoint is an opportunity for experience design
- **Pain Points**: Moments of friction, confusion, delay, or failure that reduce customer satisfaction. Primary input for redesign and technology investment prioritization
- **Moments of Truth**: The critical touchpoints that disproportionately influence overall customer perception — the first login, the billing dispute, the renewal call. These warrant more design investment than routine touchpoints
- **Current State vs. Future State**: Current-state journey maps document reality (often requires research to build accurately). Future-state maps visualize the intended experience after transformation. Both are needed — without a current-state baseline, future-state vision has no grounding
- **Persona Anchor**: Journey maps are anchored to a specific persona. The same service can produce dramatically different journeys for different user types — map separately, not averaged

## Method Application
Method builds customer journey maps at the start of customer-facing technology programs. The map provides the requirements context that functional specifications lack: not just "the system shall support X" but "at the moment a customer is trying to do Y, and they're frustrated because Z, the system needs to X." Requirements anchored to journey moments are more accurate and more accepted by business stakeholders.

## Consulting Insight
🎯 **Consulting Tool — Customer Journey Mapping**: A journey map has done its job when a stakeholder sees their customer's experience and says "I didn't know it was that bad." That moment — when the continuous customer experience is visible to teams who only own fragments of it — is the event that converts journey mapping from a UX exercise into an alignment tool. Build the map collaboratively, in a room with representatives from every team that owns a touchpoint, and the emotional curve does the persuasion work. → `consulting-tools-repository/customer-journey-mapping.md`
