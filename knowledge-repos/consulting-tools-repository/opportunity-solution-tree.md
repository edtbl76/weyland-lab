# Opportunity Solution Tree (OST)

## What It Is
A visual framework developed by Teresa Torres that structures continuous product discovery by connecting a desired outcome (top) to the opportunities that would produce it, the solutions that address those opportunities, and the experiments that validate those solutions. The tree prevents the most common product discovery failures: jumping straight from outcome to solution, treating opportunities as a flat backlog, and failing to test assumptions before building. The OST makes the team's discovery and prioritization reasoning explicit and visible — both for internal alignment and stakeholder communication.

## When to Use
- Structuring a product discovery practice for a client team
- When a team is jumping from desired outcomes directly to feature development without understanding the opportunity space
- Quarterly product planning: organizing which opportunities to explore and which solutions to test
- When product roadmap debates become political — OST grounds them in outcome and opportunity logic
- OKR alignment: mapping Key Results to the opportunity tree to ensure delivery connects to desired outcomes

## Key Concepts
- **Desired Outcome**: The root of the tree — the business and customer outcome the team is working toward. Should be a metric the team can influence, not a business result (revenue) they cannot directly control. Maps directly to the North Star Metric or an OKR Key Result
- **Opportunities**: The customer needs, pain points, desires, or context factors that, if addressed, would produce the desired outcome. Opportunities are discovered through research — interviews, contextual inquiry, analytics. They are not solutions or features
- **Opportunity Space**: The full set of discovered opportunities. The tree structure reveals which opportunities are related, which are sub-opportunities of larger ones, and which are in tension. Teams choose which branch of the tree to focus on each quarter
- **Solutions**: Proposed ways to address a specific opportunity. Multiple solutions can be generated for each opportunity — and should be before committing. Solutions are hypotheses, not commitments
- **Experiments**: The smallest test that would validate or invalidate a key assumption about a solution. Prevents building before testing. Experiments produce the evidence that informs which solutions to build
- **Continuous Discovery**: OST supports a continuous discovery cadence — at least one customer interview per week, outcomes feeding back into the tree, tree evolving as discovery accumulates. It is not a one-time planning artifact
- **Anti-pattern — Solution Tree**: Teams without OST often operate a "solution tree" — the root is a feature ("build a dashboard"), the branches are sub-features, and there are no outcomes or opportunities. This produces a well-executed delivery of the wrong thing
- **OST vs. Product Roadmap**: The roadmap communicates delivery commitments to stakeholders. The OST is the discovery and prioritization reasoning beneath the roadmap. Teams that share the OST with stakeholders create conversations about opportunity and outcome rather than feature negotiations

## Method Application
Method introduces the OST in product programs where continuous discovery needs to be established alongside delivery. It provides the structural link between OKRs (the desired outcome), research (the opportunities), solution design (the solutions), and delivery planning. The tree also creates transparency for clients: rather than "trust us, we're doing discovery," the OST shows what opportunities are being explored and why.

## Consulting Insight
🎯 **Consulting Tool — Opportunity Solution Tree**: The most common product team failure mode is a flat, undifferentiated backlog of features with no connection to outcomes or opportunities. The OST doesn't fix this by adding process — it fixes it by making the reasoning visible. When a stakeholder asks "why aren't we building X?", the answer isn't "it's not prioritized" — it's "X addresses opportunity Y, which is not in the branch of the tree we're focused on this quarter because we believe opportunity Z produces more outcome movement." That is a conversation about strategy, not a negotiation about rank. → `consulting-tools-repository/opportunity-solution-tree.md`
