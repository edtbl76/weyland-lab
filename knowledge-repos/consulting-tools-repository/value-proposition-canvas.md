# Value Proposition Canvas

## What It Is
A tool developed by Alexander Osterwalder that zooms into the Customer Segments and Value Propositions blocks of the Business Model Canvas to map the fit between what a product offers and what customers actually need. The canvas has two sides: the Customer Profile (Customer Jobs, Pains, and Gains) and the Value Map (Products & Services, Pain Relievers, and Gain Creators). Value occurs when Pain Relievers address Pains and Gain Creators deliver Gains that customers care about. The canvas is used to validate that a product concept addresses real customer needs — and to diagnose why an existing product isn't achieving the expected adoption.

## When to Use
- Validating a product concept before investing in development
- Diagnosing low adoption or customer satisfaction for an existing product
- Requirements analysis: structuring functional requirements around customer jobs and pains rather than features
- When a client's roadmap is feature-led rather than customer-needs-led
- Pitch and proposal development: articulating the value of a technology investment in customer terms

## Key Concepts
- **Customer Jobs**: What customers are trying to accomplish — functional jobs (tasks), social jobs (how they want to be perceived), and emotional jobs (feelings they want). Jobs are stated as customer goals, not product features: "manage my team's capacity" not "view a dashboard"
- **Customer Pains**: The obstacles, risks, and negative outcomes that prevent or frustrate customers from getting their jobs done. Pains vary in severity — extreme pains warrant attention; minor annoyances may not justify investment
- **Customer Gains**: The outcomes and benefits customers want — required gains (basic expectations), expected gains (typical outcomes), desired gains (beyond expectations), and unexpected gains (surprises that create delight). Maps closely to Kano's Basic/Performance/Excitement classification
- **Products & Services**: What the company offers to help customers get jobs done. Listed without claims — just the offering
- **Pain Relievers**: How specific products and services reduce or eliminate customer pains. Effective pain relievers target extreme pains — not all pains need to be addressed
- **Gain Creators**: How specific products and services produce the gains customers care about. Focus on the gains that matter most, not all gains
- **Value Proposition Fit**: Fit is achieved when Pain Relievers address ranked Pains and Gain Creators produce Gains customers care about. Problem-Solution Fit is validated conceptually; Product-Market Fit is validated in the market
- **Job as the Unit of Analysis**: The canvas works best when Customer Jobs are well-defined. Vague jobs ("manage operations") produce vague canvases. Specific jobs ("reconcile payment discrepancies before month-end close") produce actionable canvases

## Method Application
Used in requirements analysis workshops to reframe requirements from feature lists to customer-need maps. When a client comes with a list of 40 features, the Value Proposition Canvas restructures the conversation: which customer jobs do these features serve? Which pains are extreme enough to warrant investment? This focuses the requirements on the highest-value subset.

## Consulting Insight
🎯 **Consulting Tool — Value Proposition Canvas**: The diagnostic power of the canvas is in the gaps: Pain Relievers that don't address ranked Pains reveal features built for internal reasons rather than customer needs. Gain Creators that produce gains customers don't care about reveal assumption errors. Filling out both sides of the canvas and checking alignment — does each Pain Reliever map to a real Pain? — often reveals that 30-40% of a product roadmap serves unmapped jobs or addresses low-severity pains. → `consulting-tools-repository/value-proposition-canvas.md`
