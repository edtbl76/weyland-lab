# Double Diamond

## What It Is
A design process model developed by the British Design Council that structures problem-solving into four phases across two diamonds: Discover → Define (first diamond: problem space) and Develop → Deliver (second diamond: solution space). Each diamond follows a diverge-then-converge pattern: diverge to explore widely, then converge to select. The first diamond ensures teams are solving the right problem before investing in solutions. The second diamond ensures teams are building the right solution before committing to delivery. The model is visually intuitive and widely adopted as a shared mental model for design and product work across disciplines.

## When to Use
- Structuring discovery and design phases for new products or features
- When a client has jumped to a solution before understanding the problem
- Facilitating cross-functional alignment on design process (helps non-designers understand where in the process the team is)
- Scoping Method engagements: distinguishing discovery work (first diamond) from delivery work (second diamond)
- Communicating to executive stakeholders why definition work precedes solution development

## Key Concepts
- **Discover**: Divergent research phase — understand the problem landscape through user research, market analysis, stakeholder interviews, and contextual inquiry. Goal: generate insights, not solutions
- **Define**: Convergent synthesis phase — synthesize research into a clear problem statement, design brief, or "How Might We" framing. Output: a well-defined problem worth solving
- **Develop**: Divergent solution phase — generate multiple solution concepts through ideation, prototyping, and testing. Goal: explore the solution space before committing
- **Deliver**: Convergent delivery phase — select, refine, and build the best solution. Output: a tested, ready-to-implement solution
- **Problem Space vs. Solution Space**: The most common failure mode is collapsing the first diamond — assuming the problem is known and jumping directly to solution development. The Double Diamond makes this error visible
- **Diverge-Converge Pattern**: Each diamond is not linear; it requires actively resisting convergence during the divergent phase. Teams naturally want to evaluate ideas as they generate them — this collapses the diamond
- **Iteration**: In practice, the diamonds are not strictly sequential; insights from the solution space often reframe the problem space. The model is a guide, not a constraint

## Method Application
Method uses the Double Diamond as a communication tool to set expectations with clients about where discovery investment goes. When clients ask "why can't we just start building?", the Double Diamond illustrates why skipping the first diamond produces solutions to the wrong problem. Also used to scope engagements: discovery phases (first diamond) are scoped separately from delivery phases (second diamond).

## Consulting Insight
🎯 **Consulting Tool — Double Diamond**: The most valuable intervention the Double Diamond enables is convincing a client to stay in the first diamond longer than they want to. Clients under delivery pressure want to converge immediately — they have a solution in mind and want to validate it, not explore. The Double Diamond gives you a framework to explain why that's a risk: if you haven't truly defined the problem, the solution you deliver may be excellent but irrelevant. → `consulting-tools-repository/double-diamond.md`
