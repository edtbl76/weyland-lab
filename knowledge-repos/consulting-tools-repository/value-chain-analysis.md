# Value Chain Analysis

## What It Is
A framework developed by Michael Porter that disaggregates a firm's activities into primary activities (directly involved in creating and delivering the product: Inbound Logistics, Operations, Outbound Logistics, Marketing & Sales, Service) and support activities (enabling infrastructure: Firm Infrastructure, Human Resource Management, Technology Development, Procurement). Analyzing cost and differentiation potential across each activity reveals where competitive advantage is created or eroded, and where technology investment will have strategic impact vs. where it is table stakes.

## When to Use
- Technology investment prioritization: which activities create differentiation vs. which should be automated/outsourced?
- Digital transformation scoping: where in the value chain is the client losing margin or failing to differentiate?
- Build vs. buy decisions: activities that are not sources of differentiation are candidates for vendor/SaaS replacement
- Identifying where AI/automation investment yields strategic vs. operational returns
- Competitive benchmarking: compare your value chain activity costs to competitor estimates

## Key Concepts
- **Primary Activities**:
  - *Inbound Logistics*: Receiving, storing, handling inputs. Differentiation via supplier relationships and inventory management
  - *Operations*: Transforming inputs into outputs. The core production or service delivery process
  - *Outbound Logistics*: Delivering product/service to the customer. Last-mile, distribution
  - *Marketing & Sales*: Making the offering available and attractive. Brand, pricing, channels
  - *Service*: Post-sale support, maintenance, upgrades. Increasingly a margin driver in SaaS
- **Support Activities**:
  - *Technology Development*: R&D, process automation, software development. Relevant to all primary activities
  - *Human Resource Management*: Recruiting, training, compensation
  - *Procurement*: Purchasing inputs. Strategic sourcing
  - *Firm Infrastructure*: Finance, legal, quality management, general management
- **Margin**: The difference between total value created and total cost. Competitive advantage comes from performing activities at lower cost or in ways that create superior value — not just overall, but per activity
- **Linkages**: The interactions between activities are often where efficiency gains and differentiation lie. An order management system that links Marketing & Sales to Operations and Outbound Logistics simultaneously reduces cost and improves delivery accuracy
- **Value System**: Extends beyond the firm to include supplier value chains and buyer value chains. Understanding where value is created and captured across the full system informs partnership and integration strategy

## Method Application
Used in technology strategy engagements to identify which systems are "lights on" infrastructure (support undifferentiated activities — use SaaS) vs. which are strategic differentiators (build or heavily customize). A retailer's outbound logistics (last-mile delivery) is a primary activity where technology differentiation creates competitive advantage; their accounting system is not.

## Consulting Insight
🎯 **Consulting Tool — Value Chain Analysis**: Map the client's value chain before recommending technology investments — it reveals which systems are strategic and which are commodity. The activities where the client earns their margin and creates their differentiation deserve custom-built or heavily configured solutions; everything else should be bought or outsourced.
