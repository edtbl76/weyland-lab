# Ansoff Matrix

## What It Is
A strategic planning tool developed by Igor Ansoff that maps four growth strategies along two dimensions: existing vs. new products, and existing vs. new markets. The four quadrants — Market Penetration (existing products, existing markets), Market Development (existing products, new markets), Product Development (new products, existing markets), and Diversification (new products, new markets) — represent increasing levels of risk. Organizations typically pursue the lowest-risk strategy first and move toward diversification as other options mature.

## When to Use
- When a client needs to articulate their growth strategy to investors or boards
- During solutions scoping to understand which quadrant a proposed initiative falls in
- When a client is considering whether to expand into new segments vs. deepen in existing ones
- Framing technology investment decisions: is this about serving current customers better or reaching new ones?
- M&A context: acquisitions in new markets (diversification) carry higher integration risk than in existing markets

## Key Concepts
- **Market Penetration**: Sell more of what you have to who you already sell to. Lowest risk. Tactics: pricing, promotions, distribution improvements, customer retention. Technology angle: improve the core product, reduce friction in existing workflows
- **Market Development**: Bring existing products to new customer segments or geographies. Medium risk — the product is known; the market is not. Technology angle: localization, new distribution channels, partner integrations
- **Product Development**: Create new products for existing customers. Medium risk — the customer is known; the product is not. Technology angle: adjacent features, platform extension, new product lines within the brand
- **Diversification**: New products for new markets. Highest risk; least synergy with current capabilities. Related diversification (adjacent industry) is safer than unrelated. Technology angle: entirely new ventures, acquisitions
- **Risk Gradient**: Each step away from the top-left quadrant increases risk. Use this to challenge client ambitions: "You're proposing Product Development, but you don't yet have product-market fit in your existing market — should we complete Market Penetration first?"
- **Relationship to Core Competencies**: The matrix doesn't account for whether the organization has the capabilities to execute each strategy. Pair with a capability assessment when choosing between quadrants

## Method Application
Used at solutions handoff and requirements analysis to frame the scope of a client engagement. If a client's brief describes "expanding our platform to serve enterprise clients" — that's Market Development. If it describes "rebuilding our core product for better retention" — that's Market Penetration. This framing clarifies what success looks like and whether the proposed scope matches the growth strategy.

## Consulting Insight
🎯 **Consulting Tool — Ansoff Matrix**: Before scoping a digital program, place the initiative in its Ansoff quadrant — it clarifies both the risk level and the definition of success. Most technology programs that fail are misclassified: the client is attempting Product Development (new platform for existing customers) but measuring success with Market Development metrics (new customer acquisition). Getting the quadrant right aligns scope, success criteria, and risk tolerance from the start. → `consulting-tools-repository/ansoff-matrix.md`
