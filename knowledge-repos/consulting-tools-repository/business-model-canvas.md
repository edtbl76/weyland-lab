# Business Model Canvas

## What It Is
A strategic management tool developed by Alexander Osterwalder that maps a business model onto nine building blocks: Customer Segments, Value Propositions, Channels, Customer Relationships, Revenue Streams, Key Resources, Key Activities, Key Partnerships, and Cost Structure. The canvas fits on one page, making it a rapid visual tool for understanding how a business creates, delivers, and captures value. Used both for analyzing existing business models and designing new ones, the canvas is designed to be filled collaboratively — sticky notes, not slides — so the team builds shared understanding rather than receiving a completed document.

## When to Use
- Solutions handoff: understanding the client's business model before scoping technology work
- When a client's technology initiative doesn't connect clearly to a business model component
- Evaluating new product or business line investments — what's the revenue model?
- Identifying which parts of the business model the technology program is enabling, changing, or disrupting
- Startup engagements: validating business model assumptions before product investment

## Key Concepts
- **Customer Segments**: Who are the customers? B2B vs. B2C, mass market vs. niche, multi-sided platforms. Defines the "for whom"
- **Value Propositions**: What value is delivered to each segment? What problem is solved? What need is satisfied? Defines the "what"
- **Channels**: How does the value proposition reach the customer? Sales, marketing, delivery. Where does technology typically live
- **Customer Relationships**: How does the company acquire, retain, and grow customers? Personal service, self-service, automated, community
- **Revenue Streams**: How does the company earn from each segment? Transaction, subscription, licensing, advertising, freemium, usage-based
- **Key Resources**: The assets required to deliver the value proposition — physical, intellectual, human, financial. Technology platforms are often Key Resources
- **Key Activities**: The most important things the company must do to deliver the value proposition. Software development, platform operations, and data processing often appear here
- **Key Partnerships**: Who does the company need to work with to deliver? Suppliers, technology partners, channel partners. Informs build/buy/partner decisions
- **Cost Structure**: What are the most significant costs? Fixed vs. variable, economies of scale, cost-driven vs. value-driven
- **Business Model vs. Operating Model**: The BMC describes what the business does; the operating model describes how. Technology programs often change operating model components while leaving the business model intact — or vice versa
- **Value Proposition Canvas**: The BMC's Customer Segments and Value Propositions blocks are expanded into the Value Proposition Canvas for deeper customer-facing analysis

## Method Application
Method uses the Business Model Canvas at the start of client engagements to establish a shared understanding of the client's business before scoping technology work. Technology programs that can't be traced to a canvas component risk being solutions in search of a problem. The canvas also surfaces Key Partnerships that become vendor or integration considerations in the solutions phase.

## Consulting Insight
🎯 **Consulting Tool — Business Model Canvas**: Technology programs succeed when they directly enable or strengthen a specific canvas component — and fail when they operate in isolation from the business model. When scoping a program, trace the technology investment to the canvas: which Key Activities are being automated? Which Channels are being improved? If you can't draw that line, the program doesn't have a business case — it has a technology aspiration. → `consulting-tools-repository/business-model-canvas.md`
