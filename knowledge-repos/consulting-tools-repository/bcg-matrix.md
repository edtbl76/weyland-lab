# BCG Matrix

## What It Is
The Boston Consulting Group Growth-Share Matrix is a portfolio analysis tool that categorizes a company's business units or products along two dimensions: market growth rate (vertical axis) and relative market share (horizontal axis). The resulting four quadrants — Stars (high growth, high share), Cash Cows (low growth, high share), Question Marks (high growth, low share), and Dogs (low growth, low share) — provide a basis for resource allocation decisions across a portfolio. Developed by BCG in the 1970s, it remains a standard framework for portfolio strategy discussions with executives.

## When to Use
- Executive conversations about portfolio prioritization and investment allocation
- Identifying which business units or products to grow, maintain, harvest, or exit
- Pre-engagement strategy work to understand a client's competitive position
- Framing technology investment decisions across multiple product lines
- Solutions scoping when a client needs to justify build vs. retire decisions

## Key Concepts
- **Stars**: High growth, high market share. Require significant investment to maintain position; become Cash Cows when growth slows. Prioritize for continued investment
- **Cash Cows**: Low growth, high market share. Generate more cash than consumed; fund Stars and Question Marks. Protect, optimize, and milk
- **Question Marks**: High growth, low market share. Require investment to become Stars; risk becoming Dogs if market share doesn't grow. Selective investment decisions
- **Dogs**: Low growth, low market share. Often divested or maintained at minimal cost. Occasionally kept for strategic reasons (completing a portfolio, serving a customer segment)
- **Market Growth Rate**: Typically uses industry growth rate as proxy; high = above ~10% annually
- **Relative Market Share**: Share relative to the largest competitor, not absolute share. A 30% share vs. a 60% market leader = 0.5 relative share (low)
- **Limitations**: Oversimplifies competitive dynamics; ignores synergies between units; market share as a proxy for competitive advantage is imperfect. Use as a starting conversation, not a final answer
- **Cash Flow Logic**: The underlying insight is cash flow: Cash Cows generate surplus; Stars and Question Marks consume cash; Dogs are neutral or drain resources

## Method Application
Used in strategy engagements to facilitate portfolio discussions with executive stakeholders. Particularly useful in digital transformation engagements where a client has multiple products at different lifecycle stages. Helps prioritize which systems to modernize (Stars, Cash Cows) vs. sunset (Dogs). Pair with Wardley Mapping for technology-specific portfolio decisions.

## Consulting Insight
🎯 **Consulting Tool — BCG Matrix**: Use the BCG Matrix to open the portfolio prioritization conversation with executives, not to close it. The quadrant placement will be debated — that debate is the value. Most clients have Dogs they're emotionally attached to and Question Marks they're underinvesting in; the matrix makes that tension explicit. Pair with cash flow data and strategic context; don't let quadrant labels drive decisions without supporting analysis. → `consulting-tools-repository/bcg-matrix.md`
