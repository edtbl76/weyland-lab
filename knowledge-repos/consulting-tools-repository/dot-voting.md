# Dot Voting (Sticker Voting / Multi-Voting)

## What It Is
A rapid democratic prioritization technique where participants allocate a fixed number of votes (represented by dot stickers, marks, or digital equivalents) to their preferred options from a list — enabling a group to quickly identify collective priorities without extended discussion. Each participant receives a fixed dot budget (typically 3-5 dots per person) and places dots on their preferred items. Items with the most dots rise to the top. Dot voting is fast, visual, inclusive (all voices carry equal weight), and produces a clear priority signal without requiring consensus on every item. It is used to prioritize ideas, themes, risks, features, and HMW questions in workshops and planning sessions.

## When to Use
- After affinity mapping: prioritizing clusters or themes
- After ideation: selecting which ideas to develop further
- After HMW generation: selecting the highest-priority design challenges
- Any workshop situation where a group needs to converge on priorities from a large set of options
- When discussion-based prioritization is producing gridlock or dominated by one voice

## Key Concepts
- **Dot Budget**: Each participant receives the same number of votes — typically 3-5. Fewer dots force harder prioritization; more dots produce a flatter distribution. A rule of thumb: number of items / 4, rounded up, produces reasonable differentiation
- **All Dots, All Items**: Participants can distribute their dots any way they choose — concentrate all on one item or spread across many. This allows intensity of preference to be expressed
- **Silent Voting**: Participants vote simultaneously, silently, without explaining their choices. Discussion after the vote, not during. This prevents anchoring effects where early voters influence later ones
- **Result Interpretation**: Dot voting identifies the top priorities, not a single winner. The result is typically 2-3 items that cluster at the top with significant separation from the rest. These become the focus; the rest are archived
- **Facilitated vs. Unfacilitated Voting**: When power dynamics are present (executives and junior staff in the same room), consider anonymous voting tools (Mentimeter, digital boards) or having the facilitator collect and display results before participants see each other's choices
- **Not a Decision**: Dot voting is a fast prioritization signal, not a binding decision. The result should inform but not replace structured decision-making for high-stakes choices. "The dots say X" is input, not output
- **Dot Voting for Affinity Clusters**: When used after affinity mapping, each participant votes on the clusters (themes) they consider most important to address. The resulting priority order drives the HMW generation and subsequent ideation
- **Digital Equivalents**: Emoji reactions, thumbs-up counts, poll tools, and digital sticky note vote counts replicate dot voting in virtual workshops. The principle is the same; the medium is different

## Method Application
Method uses dot voting as the standard convergence technique in workshops following divergent phases — after brainstorming, after affinity mapping, after HMW generation. It takes 5-10 minutes, produces an immediate visual result, and shifts group energy from generation to selection without prolonged debate. It is the most efficient convergence tool in a facilitation toolkit for groups of 3-20 people.

## Consulting Insight
🎯 **Consulting Tool — Dot Voting**: The facilitation failure to avoid with dot voting is interpreting the result as a decision rather than a signal. When the highest-dotted item is the HiPPO's preference, or when the facilitator has a preferred outcome, the dots can be rationalized to confirm what was already decided. Use dot voting to surface the group's genuine collective priority, then validate the result against strategic criteria before committing.
