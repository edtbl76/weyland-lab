# Blue Ocean Strategy

## What It Is
A strategy framework by W. Chan Kim and Renée Mauborgne that advocates creating uncontested market space ("blue oceans") rather than competing in existing overcrowded markets ("red oceans"). While traditional strategy focuses on outcompeting rivals within an existing industry, Blue Ocean Strategy focuses on value innovation — simultaneously reducing cost and increasing buyer value by redefining industry boundaries. The central tool is the ERRC Grid (Eliminate-Reduce-Raise-Create), used to redesign the value curve relative to the competitive norm.

## When to Use
- When a client's existing market is highly competitive with compressed margins (red ocean conditions)
- Product strategy engagements where the goal is differentiation, not incremental improvement
- Innovation workshops where the brief is to identify new growth vectors outside the current competitive set
- When a client wants to challenge industry assumptions about what customers value
- Framing a platform or ecosystem play that creates new market categories

## Key Concepts
- **Red Ocean vs. Blue Ocean**: Red oceans are existing industries with defined rules and competition for market share. Blue oceans are new market spaces where demand is created rather than contested. Companies like Cirque du Soleil, Netflix, and Airbnb created blue oceans by redefining their industry's value curve
- **Value Innovation**: The simultaneous pursuit of differentiation and low cost — not a tradeoff. Achieved by eliminating and reducing factors the industry competes on while raising and creating factors not previously offered
- **ERRC Grid**:
  - *Eliminate*: Which factors the industry takes for granted should be eliminated? (Remove cost without reducing buyer value)
  - *Reduce*: Which factors should be reduced well below the industry standard?
  - *Raise*: Which factors should be raised well above the industry standard?
  - *Create*: Which factors should be created that the industry has never offered?
- **Strategy Canvas**: A diagnostic tool that plots the investment level across competitive factors for your offering vs. competitors. A divergent value curve (different from competitors) with a compelling tagline signals a potential blue ocean
- **Six Paths Framework**: Six approaches to reconstructing industry boundaries: Look Across Alternative Industries, Strategic Groups, Buyer Groups, Complementary Products/Services, Functional-Emotional Appeal, and Time (trends)
- **Non-Customers**: Blue Ocean focuses on the three tiers of non-customers (soon-to-be, refusing, unexplored) rather than existing customers. The largest unmet demand often lies with people who have rejected the current industry offering

## Method Application
Used in innovation strategy and product strategy engagements when the client is asking "where should we compete next?" or "how do we differentiate when the market is commoditizing?" The ERRC Grid is particularly effective in workshops — it forces concrete trade-offs rather than additive feature lists. Pair with customer research (contextual inquiry, JTBD) to validate which factors to eliminate vs. create.

## Consulting Insight
🎯 **Consulting Tool — Blue Ocean Strategy**: The most powerful use of Blue Ocean in consulting is the ERRC Grid run as a workshop. It forces clients to make subtractive decisions — what to stop doing — which is harder and more valuable than deciding what to add. Most product roadmaps only Raise and Create; the Eliminate and Reduce quadrants unlock the cost reductions that fund differentiation. Push clients to name at least one thing to Eliminate; if they can't, they're iterating on a red ocean strategy. → `consulting-tools-repository/blue-ocean-strategy.md`
