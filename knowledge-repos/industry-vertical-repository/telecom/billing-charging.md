# Billing & Charging

## What It Is
Telecom billing and charging is the system that measures customer usage, rates it against applicable tariffs, generates invoices, and manages the revenue lifecycle from service activation through payment collection. It is the most complex billing domain in any industry — handling multiple charging models (prepaid, postpaid, usage-based), real-time event rating, convergent billing across fixed and mobile services, and the regulatory taxation requirements of every jurisdiction in which the operator serves customers.

## Why It Matters in Telecom
Billing is both the operator's revenue system and the customer's primary touchpoint for understanding their service costs. Billing errors erode customer trust and generate high call center volume. Revenue leakage (usage that is not correctly rated and charged) directly reduces margin. Billing system modernization is one of the most common and highest-stakes IT programs in telecoms — legacy systems (Amdocs, Convergys, CSG, Ericsson) are deeply entrenched and extremely difficult to replace while maintaining service continuity.

## Key Concepts
- **Mediation**: The collection, normalization, and aggregation of raw usage records (CDRs, xDRs) from network elements. Mediation translates network-specific formats into standardized records that the rating engine can process. Data completeness and latency in mediation directly affect billing accuracy.
- **CDR / xDR (Call/Event Detail Record)**: The raw usage event records generated by network elements — a call record (CDR), a data session record, an SMS record. These are the inputs to the rating engine.
- **Rating Engine**: The component that applies tariff rules to usage events to calculate charges. Rating must handle complex tariff structures — bundles (included minutes, data allowances), tiered pricing, time-of-day rates, promotional discounts, roaming rates, and intercarrier settlement rates.
- **Real-Time Charging (OCS)**: Online Charging System — the real-time credit control function for prepaid customers and convergent real-time charging. OCS intercepts each usage event, checks the customer's balance or allowance, authorizes the event, and deducts the appropriate charge in real time. 3GPP Diameter (Gy interface) is the standard protocol. Latency requirements are strict — OCS must respond within the call setup time.
- **Offline Charging (OFCS)**: Batch-oriented charging for postpaid customers. CDRs accumulate during the billing cycle and are rated in batch at billing time. Less latency-sensitive than OCS.
- **Convergent Billing**: A single billing system handling all service types (mobile voice, data, fixed broadband, IPTV, IoT) for a customer. Technically complex because each service type has different CDR formats, rating rules, and regulatory treatment.
- **Billing Cycle / Invoice Generation**: The process of aggregating rated charges for a billing period, applying taxes and discounts, generating an invoice document, and delivering it to the customer. Invoice generation at operator scale (millions of customers) requires high-throughput batch processing.
- **Revenue Assurance**: The discipline of detecting and correcting revenue leakage — usage that is not mediated, not rated, or not billed correctly. Revenue assurance requires end-to-end reconciliation between network usage data, mediation output, and billing records.
- **Interconnect / Roaming Settlement**: Charges between operators for calls/data that transit or terminate on another network. GSMA TAP (Transferred Account Procedure) and NRTRDE for roaming; SS7 CDRs for interconnect. Settlement calculation, dispute management, and payment processing are separate functions within billing.

## Common Patterns / Gotchas
- **Tariff complexity is the primary source of billing defects.** Modern telecom tariffs are deeply nested — bundles within bundles, promotional overlays, loyalty discounts, roaming rate tables. Rating logic that correctly applies every applicable tariff in the right precedence order for every possible customer configuration is extremely complex. Tariff modeling must be a dedicated workstream.
- **Mediation data quality determines billing accuracy.** Missed CDRs, duplicates, and late arrivals create billing gaps and credits. Mediation reconciliation — comparing network element counts against received CDR counts — is a mandatory operational function.
- **Real-time charging latency is a hard constraint.** OCS response time must fit within the Diameter credit control round-trip budget — typically 200–500ms for voice, less for data session authorization. OCS performance testing at peak concurrent session load is required before production launch.
- **Billing migration is a long pole program.** Replacing a legacy billing system while keeping customers billing correctly is one of the hardest IT programs in telecoms. Parallel run (running old and new systems simultaneously and reconciling differences) is standard practice and adds significant program cost.
- **Tax engine integration is mandatory.** Telecom taxation is jurisdictionally complex — federal USF, state PUC surcharges, municipal 911 fees, and more. Tax engine vendors (Avalara, Vertex O Series, CSC Corptax) handle this complexity; building tax logic in-house is strongly inadvisable.

## Solutions Context
**Typical engagement patterns**: BSS/billing modernization, real-time charging (OCS) implementation, convergent billing platform, mediation platform, revenue assurance, MVNO billing platform.

**Common scope anchors**: Mediation pipeline, rating engine, OCS (real-time charging), tariff configuration model, invoice generation, tax engine integration, revenue assurance reconciliation, interconnect/roaming settlement.

**Risk factors**: Legacy billing system data migration is the highest-risk workstream in any BSS program. OCS performance under peak concurrent session load must be validated before go-live. Tariff model complexity grows with every new product launch — scope tariff governance alongside the technical platform.
